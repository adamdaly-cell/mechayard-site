THE IDEA

The 24 Hours of Lemons is endurance racing for cars that should have been scrapped. The rules cap what the car can cost, the judges reward a good theme, and the whole thing is more about surviving the weekend than winning it.

This one cost $500. Over four years it raced as three different cars, with the same number on the door each time.

CHAPTER 01 · SEP 2012

A $500 BMW

It started as a tired black E30 coupe, bought for $500 and dragged home on a rented trailer. The 24 Hours of Lemons has a budget limit, so the car had to be cheap, and this one was.

Before any paint went on, the livery was worked out the easy way: on an E30 in Forza Motorsport.

CHAPTER 02 · APR 2013

The green tank

Lemons rewards a theme, so the E30 became a tank. It went army green with a turret box on the roof, a gun barrel over the windshield labelled THIS WAY TOWARD ENEMY, a star on the hood and the number 30 on the doors, racing as Panzer Racing.

Its first race was in April 2013.

CHAPTER 03 · 2013 – 2015

Crashes and repairs

Endurance racing on a budget is hard on a car. The tank came home with its doors and quarter panel pushed in, and in November 2013 a piston melted, leaving the cylinder head full of aluminium.

A bigger hit in July 2014 folded the front wing and driver’s door. Over the winter it got another engine, and by April 2015 it was straight again and back on its wheels.

CHAPTER 04 · 2015

Turtle Head Racing

For 2015 the tank was retired and the same green car came back as Turtle Head Racing: yellow cartoon turtles, “GOTTA GO!” down the rear quarter, a Fear the Turtle flag over the pit and a driver in a turtle suit.

CHAPTER 05 · 2016

TDI Bros: the Dieselgate spoof

In 2016, a few months after Volkswagen’s diesel emissions scandal, the car went cream and was rebranded as an “EPA Test Vehicle”: a VW roundel on the hood, a Volkswagen badge on the trunk, a TOOLE EDITION roof sign and a smoke machine for the full diesel effect.

It raced as the TDI Bros, with a BMX bike on the roof rack.
