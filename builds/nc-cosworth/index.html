WHY A SUPERCHARGER

After two years of bolt-ons the next step was power. The Cosworth supercharger was the kit for the NC at the time: a Magnuson MP62 with an air-to-water intercooler built into the case, about 8 psi on its own pulley.

I ended up installing it on my car several times and on customer cars after that, and I rewrote its instructions along the way. This era taught me most of what I know about tuning these engines, mainly by getting it wrong.

CHAPTER 01 · 2008 – 2010

Fixing the instructions

The install guide that shipped with the kit had enough errors to cause a lot of head scratching. At Cosworth's request I sent them a marked-up revision B, and offered the same file to anyone on the forum who sent me an email address. Cosworth later told me they were updating it.

After installing it several more times I corrected the instructions again and drew a hose routing diagram, because the mess of hoses is where most people got stuck.

A few tips from those installs: pull the hood, it is only four bolts and about 10 lb and it goes back on aligned. Use the supplied low-profile power steering banjo bolt, or the stock one can punch a hole in the hood. To bleed the intercooler pump, lift the reservoir as high as it goes, fill it and run the pump.

CHAPTER 02 · OCT – NOV 2009

Choosing a compression ratio

When the engine needed rebuilding I had to choose between 10.8:1 and 9.5:1 pistons. With 10.8 and 8 psi the effective compression was about 16.6:1; with 9.5 I could run more boost for the same knock margin.

I spent about 20 hours reading and asking. I went with 9.5:1 and water/methanol injection.

CHAPTER 03 · DEC 2009 – MAR 2010

The Cosworth-built engine

The new engine came back from Cosworth with their forged rods, a Mazda forged crank, Cosworth 9.5:1 forged pistons and Cosworth cams. I dropped it in by myself with the header already bolted on, so I could get a torque wrench on the header bolts. That is a lot of twisting and tilting for one person.

Cosworth's break-in: 20W-50 mineral oil and a Mazda filter, prime the oil system before first start, hold 2,000 rpm for 10 to 15 minutes, then stay under 4,000 rpm for about 1,000 miles.

THE SNAG

The timing was off. Cosworth had put an 09 crank in my 06 block and indexed it with the 06 pin.

THE FIX

Flyin' Miata and I found it independently. I made the correct index pin using a dial indicator.

CHAPTER 04 · MAR – MAY 2010

16 psi

With a forged engine it seemed silly to stay at 8 psi. A $75 aluminium 2.8 inch pulley from pulleyboys.com replaced the 3.3 inch one and made 14 psi at first, later 16.5. The 2.8 inch pulley needs a new belt; a 3.0 will run on the stock one.

I trailered the car from Michigan to Sniper in Sanford, Florida, and spent two days on Pat's Mustang dyno. The injectors were at their limit and fuel pressure fell from 58 to 53 psi at full boost, so we added a MagnaVolt to boost the fuel pump voltage. It left running well up top with a big hole in the mid range.

THE SNAG

Back home, intake air temperatures hit the 150s °F. The ECU richened short-term fuel trims by 25%, and some days the car felt slower than stock.

THE FIX

550 cc Ford injectors, flow-matched, and boost back down to 11 or 12 psi to keep the heat under control.

CHAPTER 06 · MAR – NOV 2011

Oil, carbon and the EGR

A friend's supercharger had eaten a bolt, so I opened it up to see the insides. It was full of oil, and when I washed my own I could not believe how much oil was in it too. An oily intercooler is not doing much cooling.

I added a PCV catch can and blocked off the EGR by welding the end of the supplied pipe and fitting a plate over the port. It throws a code every other start, but the carbon and hot exhaust are no longer coating the intercooler.

CHAPTER 07 · 2009 – 2012

Two engines

This setup went through two engines: the original one, then the Cosworth-built one. Looking back, I am sure I did not tighten the crank bolt enough when I assembled the second engine on a stand by myself.

In 2012 the car moved on to a turbo, on its third engine. I kept working on supercharged cars for customers, and still like how they respond: a squirt of throttle kicks the back end out.
