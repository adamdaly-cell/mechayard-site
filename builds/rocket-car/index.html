THE REQUEST

My wife was throwing a space party and asked if I could make a motorized spaceship for it.

I went a bit crazy with the request. I bought a 1996 Miata, five oil drums and some plywood, and went to work. Eight weeks later there was a white, NASA-badged rocket in the driveway that could drive down the road and blow fog out of its tail.

It made the party, then the autocross, then a summer of parades. Seven years later it came back as a carrot.

CHAPTER 01 · MAY 2016

Strip it to the frame

The donor was a tired 1996 Miata. I wanted its engine, gearbox, subframes and suspension, and nothing else.

Within a few days the body was off and cut into pieces small enough for a trailer. The last of the tub left the shop behind the lawn tractor. The rear subframe came out as one unit with the diff and suspension still on it, and every arm got cleaned, painted and fitted with new red poly bushings while it was apart.

CHAPTER 02 · MAY – JUN 2016

A frame of its own

With no body there was nothing left to hold the car together, so it got a steel ladder frame. Two long rails run front to back, with the original strut towers bolted on so the Miata suspension sits exactly where Mazda put it.

The engine and gearbox went in first as a mock-up between the subframes. By 9 June it was a rolling chassis, and the next day the radiator was mounted up front.

CHAPTER 03 · JUN 2016

Five oil drums

The fuselage is five 55-gallon oil drums. Some stayed round and became the body around the seat and tail. Others were cut into strips and pulled in to make the tapers, over a skeleton of steel strap with a domed cap for the nose.

The cockpit is a drum cut open behind a sheet-metal bulkhead, with a Sparco seat dropped in. The steering column had to dodge the body, so it is linked down to the rack with roller chain. By the end of June the whole fuselage was sitting on the chassis.

CHAPTER 04 · LATE JUN 2016

Fins, paint and smoke

The plywood came in here. Four fins were cut, test-fitted in bare wood, and then everything went white on 28 June. A NASA worm logo went on the tall fin, USA down the nose, a red disc in the tail and a roll hoop behind the seat.

A rocket needs exhaust. The tail drum hides a fog machine running off an inverter, so it can lay down a cloud on command without anything actually being on fire.

CHAPTER 05 · JUL 2016

Launch

It drove down the road for the first time on 29 June, about eight weeks after the Miata came apart, and it was ready for the space party. It ended up parked by the pond, next to a smaller rocket floating on the water.

A week later it went to the autocross. It shared the paddock with my blue NC and laid smoke on the course.

CHAPTER 06 · SUMMER 2016

Parade duty

Its first parade was around the Fourth of July in 2016, carrying “Jim Lilly for State Representative” signs for his campaign. The driver wore full astronaut gear, white suit and blue helmet, and the fog machine ran down the route.

CHAPTER 07 · MAY 2023

Second life: the carrot car

By 2023 the rocket had been sitting for a while, nose off. Then it got a new job. The fins came off and leafy green tops on long poles went on in their place, the drums and nose were covered in orange with green stripes, and it got a CAROT CAR plate.

Same Miata engine, same ladder frame, same Sparco seat. It made its parade debut as a carrot on 13 May 2023, and lined up next to the Oscar Mayer Wienermobile.
