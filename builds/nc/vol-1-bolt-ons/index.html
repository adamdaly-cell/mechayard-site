WHERE IT STARTED

The car is a 2006 MX-5 Sport with the limited-slip diff. For the first two years it got the usual bolt-ons: body parts, wheels, suspension, exhaust and pads.

The part I still like about this era is the testing. I took the car back to stock to measure what each exhaust and intake part did to noise, and raced a stock car to see what the full exhaust did to acceleration.

CHAPTER 01 · JUL – SEP 2006

Aero kit and a spoiler hunt

The Mazda aero kit went on in about eight hours: front fascia extension, side skirts, rear valance and spoiler, plus the shift knob, parking brake handle and dash trim. Mazda had already marked the drill points on the factory bumpers, which keeps the alignment honest.

The side skirts are full replacements and take about 20 minutes. Afterwards I spent a while deciding between the Mazdaspeed spoiler and the accessory one. The Mazdaspeed one costs about twice as much and needs 8 to 10 holes; the accessory spoiler needs one.

CHAPTER 02 · MAR – AUG 2007

Grilles

First came a thin billet grille from Moss. I threw out the supplied hardware because it looked like it would rust and used stainless cap-head screws from the hobby shop.

Later I swapped to a Racing Beat billet grille. Most of the forum hated how it looked and my wife loved it, so it stayed. Eventually I painted it black, which I think finally got it right.

CHAPTER 03 · MAR 2007

What do the parts actually do?

Before going further I put the car back to stock and tested each stage: Roadster Sport muffler, then the RS mid-pipe, then the Mazdaspeed cold air intake. I recorded sound levels in the cabin and in drive-bys at each stage.

The intake made no measurable difference to intake air temperature. It does make a great noise, so it went straight back on. I started calling it the Tubular Intake Resonator.

CHAPTER 04 · MAY – JUN 2007

Öhlins and Enkeis

Before the Öhlins the car had Mazdaspeed springs and sway bars on Bilsteins. It was quicker than people expected until about eight-tenths, then the chassis got upset and it felt like changing direction on marbles.

The Öhlins DFV Track coilovers cost about $2,500. I set them 1.25 inches lower than the Mazdaspeed springs, which left the ride height at 13 inches front and 13.5 rear, hub centre to fender. On the street they ride about the same as before; it is the ugly bumps where they feel more solid and connected. At Gingerman I ran 2 clicks front and 5 rear.

The wheels are Enkei RS+M, 17×8 with a 48 mm offset, from Good-Win Racing, with 235/40-17 Toyo RA-1s for the track. I went with 17s over 18s because they are lighter and more compliant. I rolled the fenders to be safe.

THE SNAG

Right after the alignment the car steered left on throttle and right off it.

THE FIX

An 8 psi difference between the rear tires. Cheapest fix ever.

CHAPTER 05 · SEP 2007

Brake pads that survive the track

The stock pads are great for a stock pad, but they cannot handle track heat. I went through three sets of them and a set of Hawk HPS before settling on Maruha SS-27s.

They feel like stock on the street after the first cold stop or two. After several autocrosses, track days and about 6,000 miles they were still great. For autocross alone the stock pads are fine; for mixed use the Maruhas are the ones.

CHAPTER 06 · JUN 2008

Full exhaust, measured

With a header, mid-pipe and Roadster Sport Q muffler, I ran roll-on races in 4th and 6th gear against a friend's stock NC, and swapped the camera man between cars to rule out weight.

The full exhaust made a clear difference in the mid range. At the top end, and in standing-start drag races, there was almost no difference at all. Not what I expected.
