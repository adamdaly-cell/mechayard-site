THE IDEA

A full-size school bus is a cheap, strong, well-maintained shell with a big diesel up front. Take the seats out, raise the roof, and it becomes whatever you want: in this case a party bus in the front and a garage for a dirt bike in the back.

It took from September 2016 to the summer of 2019, between other projects. In November 2019 it went to a new family, who flew in, picked it up and drove it home across the country.

CHAPTER 01 · SEP 2016

Retired from the route

It started as a retired yellow school bus: an International 3800 with a Carpenter body and the 7.3 litre T444E turbo diesel, with an automatic gearbox.

It came home in September 2016 and was parked next to the shop. Within a week the seats were out and the ceiling panels were down, leaving a bare steel shell with the ribs showing.

CHAPTER 02 · SEP – OCT 2016

Raising the roof

A school bus is built for kids, so the first big job was headroom. The roof was cut free along both sides and above the windshield, lifted on the existing pillars, and new steel was let in to fill the gap.

The awkward part is the front, where a raised roof has to blend back into the original front cap. I worked that transition out over a photo of the bus in CAD before cutting anything, then built it on scaffolding. By the middle of October the new roof was on and skinned.

CHAPTER 03 · OCT – NOV 2016

Not yellow any more

A school bus can’t stay yellow on the road, so it went orange. Everything was masked, the body was painted in the driveway, and the bumpers and wheels went light grey.

It was orange in time for a photo next to the other big project of 2016, the Rocket Car.

CHAPTER 04 · NOV 2016 – JUN 2017

Inside the shell

The inside got closed-cell spray foam over the whole roof and walls, then white panels over the ribs, with gaps left for wiring and a roof vent.

At the back, the emergency door became the start of a garage: the rear wall was opened up and a heavy steel hinge line went in for a fold-down ramp.

CHAPTER 05 · 2017

Power, storage and air

Underneath, steel cages went into the space between the frame rails. One carries the generator and its fuel can, another is an open storage box with the shore-power inlet beside it.

A window air conditioner sits in its own sheet-metal box let into the side of the bus. By the autumn of 2017 it was going on road trips.

CHAPTER 06 · AUG 2019

Garage in the back, lounge up front

Finished, the back of the bus is a garage: the ramp folds down and a dirt bike rolls straight in. The breaker panel and inverter are on the garage wall.

Up front is the lounge: two dinettes with grey tables and orange cushions, and the driver’s seat. The orange fabric is surplus from Herman Miller, the office furniture maker in our town, which sells its leftovers cheap. The original STOP arm hangs on the back wall.

I built it to be modular, so things can be added or removed without affecting everything else. The overhead cabinets come down in two-door sections, so a shower could go in up to the ceiling. The space under the seats and bed was built to hold large water tanks, with an easy path for the plumbing.
